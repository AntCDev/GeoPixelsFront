Rule Nuances & Clarifications

The canvas is a dynamic space, and some situations require more context than the main rules provide. These points clarify our moderation stance on complex topics.

1. Global Rule Enforcement

The Canvas and Beyond: All rules applicable to the main canvas apply equally to every aspect of the site. This includes art uploaded to profile pictures, profile banners, and text within social media fields.

Violations found on a user profile are enforced with the same severity as violations on the world map.

2. Art Quality & The Hierarchy of Overwriting

To manage space fairly, we define art by three levels of quality. This hierarchy helps determine what constitutes "griefing" versus "cleaning."

  • Noise (Lowest Quality): This includes scribbles, random pixel patterns, or voids. Users are free to delete or clean these to tidy up an area. This is not griefing. However, systematically following a user to remove their "noise" wherever they move constitutes harassment.
  • Low Quality: This includes repetitive sprites, generic logos (gaming/sports teams), flags, or simple standalone text. These should not be removed without reason. However, if a High Quality piece needs space in a contested area, it is acceptable to overwrite Low Quality art, though users should attempt to accommodate the original art elsewhere first.
  • High Quality: Art where time, effort, and clear artistic intent are visible. These pieces are protected by standard griefing rules.

Spam & Visual Noise:

  • Spam occurs when a user or group places the exact same sprite, image, or artwork repeatedly across a specific location with little or no variation.
  • While simple pieces—such as a small national flag or a basic 8-bit icon—are perfectly fine in isolation, excessive repetition creates visual clutter. If this happens in busy or crowded areas of the map, those repeated elements will be downgraded to the Noise level, meaning they can be freely cleaned up or overwritten by others to reclaim canvas space.
  • While the simpler the art piece, the more likely that its repetition will be considered spam, ultimately determining when repeated art crosses the line from an acceptable presence to disruptive spam is entirely at the moderation team's discretion.

3. Edits, Signs, & Author Intent

Determining Intent: Explicit signage is the clearest indicator of an artist's wishes, but actions also convey intent. If the original artist removes an edit or addition you made, you must respect that decision. Re-adding an edit after the author has cleaned it is considered harassment.

Reporting Protocol:

  • Subjective Edits (Artist Only): For edits that technically constitute "art" but may be unwanted (e.g., adding accessories, changing colors, unwanted "spade-ing"), only the original artist should report the issue. Third parties and moderators cannot know if these edits were consensual collaboration or unwanted interference.
  • Objective Griefing (Public): For obvious rule violations such as scribbling, destruction, or hate speech, anyone can and should report the activity.

Direction of Edits:

  • SFW on NSFW (Censorship): Do not edit explicit art to make it SFW (e.g., covering parts up) unless the author requested it. Censoring an artist's intended work is considered griefing.
  • NSFW on SFW: Avoid adding explicit elements to fully SFW pieces unless the author has stated otherwise.
  • Thematic Edits: Innocuous edits that match the theme, or adding explicit details to pieces that are already explicit/suggestive, are generally acceptable parts of collaboration.

Third-Party Disputes:

Conflicts involving third parties—such as one user cleaning up another user's edit over a piece that belongs to neither of them—are considered part of the natural flow of the canvas. In these instances, neither user is inherently "in the right," and the moderation team will generally not intervene.

Exceptions may be made for special circumstances (for example, a user explicitly acting on the verified will of the original artist), but these situations must be clearly defined and communicated to the moderation team (e.g., via Discord). Unless specific context is provided to and accepted by the team, these edit conflicts are not reportable.

4. Official & Community Zones

Official SFW Zones: These areas are strictly enforced. Any suggestive or explicit content found here will be moved or deleted, and repeat offenders will be punished.

  • Spawn Area: Located at coordinates [107958,196137] and its surroundings.
  • More zones may be added if the previous zones sees enough activity and enough demand to create another.

Unofficial Community Zones: Users often create organic zones (e.g., specific fandoms or NSFW clusters). While rules here are not strictly enforced by mods, disrupting established zones is poor etiquette. Your history of disrupting these zones may be used as context when judging future reports.

5. Context for Fetish vs. Hate Speech

Provocative speech (such as self-degradation or raceplay) is only permitted if it is clearly contextualized as a fetish.

The High Quality Requirement: To distinguish fetish content from genuine hate speech, the context must be established through High Quality art pieces. Adding low-effort context (like small hearts or scribbles) to shield hate speech is not permitted. If the art surrounding the speech is "Noise" or "Low Quality," the text will likely be judged as hate speech standing on its own.

6. General Provocative Speech

Outside of specific fetish contexts, we distinguish between provocative expression and incitement to hate. The defining line is whether a phrase constitutes a static statement or an active call to action.

The General Rule: You are free to make neutral, positive, or even inflammatory statements regarding subjects or beliefs. However, speech that explicitly excludes groups, negates their human worth, or calls for active harm is forbidden.

  • Allowed (Statements/Affirmations): "[Group] Lives Matter" (Positive Affirmation), "[Group] Extinction" (A provocative or fetishistic description of a scenario/state).
  • Not Allowed (Exclusion/Calls to Action): "Only [Group] Lives Matter" (Exclusionary), "[Group] Lives Don't Matter" (Dehumanization), "[Group] Genocide" (Implies active violence or a command to harm).

7. National, Religious, & Ideological Symbols

Symbols of national, religious, or political identity are permitted. However, if a symbol is used as part of a larger piece that constitutes hate speech or harassment, the entire artwork will be subject to moderation.

This allowance does not extend to symbols that advocate for real-world illegal acts. Flags or symbols associated with pedophilia (e.g., the MAP flag) or zoophilia are strictly forbidden and will be removed on sight.

8. Fiction vs. Reality

For many sensitive topics, the most critical factor is the line between fiction and reality. Artistic, stylized, and clearly fictional depictions of taboo subjects are generally allowed.

Our rules become strict when content blurs this line. Anything that promotes, advocates for, or could be mistaken for real-world illegal acts, abuse, or hate is strictly prohibited and subject to removal.

9. Age-Related & Non-Human Content

Following the 'Fiction vs. Reality' principle, stylized fictional depictions of youth (e.g., 'lolicon', 'shota', 'toddlercon') and non-human subjects (e.g., 'furry', 'feral') are permitted.

All such art must be clearly fantastical and not photorealistic. Any content that blurs this line or could be mistaken for real-world abuse will be removed.

Any mention or depiction that promotes or glorifies real-world pedophilia or zoophilia is strictly forbidden. This includes using associated flags or baselessly accusing other users of being pedophiles.

10. Violence, Gore, & Sexual Assault

Representing disabled people (e.g., with prosthetics, wheelchairs) is perfectly acceptable and is not considered gore.

Extreme themes leaning into BDSM, CNC (consensual non-consent), Ryona, or fictional sexual assault (SA) are allowed as long as they are clearly artistic, not based on any real person, and not hyper-realistic. The line is crossed with gratuitous, realistic gore or content that promotes real-world violence. Art featuring visible innards or detailed dismemberment intended primarily for shock value is subject to removal.

11. "Gross" & Shock Content

Most fetish content, including watersports, is allowed. For particularly niche or potentially unsettling themes (e.g., scat, diapers), it's best to create a dedicated zone away from more general art areas.

The key is artistic and sexual intent versus pure shock value. A sexually-themed diaper drawing is likely fine. A hyper-realistic depiction of feces with no artistic or fetish context is not and will be removed.

This is a Living Document

More nuances will be added over time as the canvas evolves and new situations arise. Moderation on these topics can be subjective. We encourage you to join our Discord and share your feedback in the rules discussion channel if you feel we are being too lenient or too strict. Ultimately, the interpretations listed here are final and must be respected.
